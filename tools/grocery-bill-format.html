What a grocery bill should show

Whether you call it a grocery bill format, a kirana store bill or a general store bill book, the same details belong on every bill:

  1. Your shop name, address and phone, and your GSTIN if you are registered for GST
  2. A bill number in running order (GB-001, GB-002 and so on) and the date
  3. The customer's name if you keep a customer list or give credit (udhaar)
  4. Each item with quantity, unit, rate before GST and the GST rate that applies to it
  5. The GST amount, split into CGST and SGST for a sale within the state
  6. The total and the payment mode (cash, UPI, card or udhaar)

If you are not registered for GST you cannot charge it, so you issue a plain bill with no tax and no GSTIN.

GST rates differ item by item

There is no single GST rate for groceries. Since the rate changes of 22 September 2025, for example, UHT milk is nil-rated, sugar, butter, ghee, biscuits, chocolates and toilet soap are at 5%, and aerated soft drinks are at 40%. The rate can depend on the exact product and its packaging, so check each item (by its HSN code) before you put it in your catalogue. A billing app that stores the GST rate against each item saves you from choosing it again at the counter.

Tax invoice or bill of supply?

A tax invoice shows GST and is issued by a registered shop on taxable goods. A bill of supply has no tax on it. Composition dealers cannot collect GST from customers, so they issue a bill of supply, and so does anyone selling exempt goods. If you sell both taxable and exempt items, issue a tax invoice for the taxable goods and a bill of supply for the exempt goods.

A supplier of goods must register for GST once yearly turnover goes above ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh in special category states). Check any price with the free GST calculator, and for business-to-business sales use the GST invoice generator to get a PDF.

Mistakes to avoid on a grocery bill

  • Using one GST rate for the whole bill. Rates differ by item, so show them line by line.
  • Charging GST when you are not registered. An unregistered shop cannot collect it.
  • Giving a tax invoice from a composition shop. Composition dealers issue a bill of supply.
  • Skipping bill numbers. Keep one running series so no bill goes missing.
  • Forgetting udhaar entries. Note the customer and the payment mode on every credit sale.

Frequently asked questions

What should a grocery bill include?

The shop name, address and phone, a running bill number and the date, each item with quantity, rate and GST rate, the total, and the payment mode. If you are GST-registered, add your GSTIN and show the tax split. A shop that sells at the counter to walk-in customers can keep customer details short.

Do I need to charge GST on every grocery item?

No. GST rates differ by item. Since 22 September 2025, for example, UHT milk is at 0% while sugar, butter, ghee and biscuits are at 5%, and aerated soft drinks are at 40%. Check the rate for each product, because it can depend on the exact item and its packaging.

What is a bill of supply and when do I use it?

A bill of supply is a bill with no GST on it. Composition dealers and sellers of exempt goods issue one. A shop that sells both taxable and exempt items issues a tax invoice for the taxable goods and a bill of supply for the exempt goods.

When must a kirana store register for GST?

A supplier of goods must register when yearly turnover goes above ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh in special category states). Suppliers who sell goods across state lines must register regardless of turnover. Check with your CA for your own case.

Can I use this template if I am not registered for GST?

Yes. Set every GST % to 0 and leave the GSTIN blank. The template then shows a plain bill with no tax.
