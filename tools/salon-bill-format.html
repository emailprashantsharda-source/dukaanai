What a salon bill should show

Whether you call it a salon bill format, a parlour bill format or a beauty parlour bill book, the same details belong on every bill:

  1. Your salon name, address and phone, and your GSTIN if you are registered for GST
  2. A bill number in running order (SB-001, SB-002 and so on) and the date
  3. The customer's name and, if you keep a customer list, a phone number
  4. Each service with the quantity and the price before GST, and the stylist who did it
  5. Any discount, shown as a separate line
  6. The GST amount, split into CGST and SGST for a customer in the same state
  7. The total and the payment mode (cash, UPI or card)

If you are not registered for GST you cannot charge it, so you issue a plain bill with no tax and no GSTIN.

GST on salon services: 5% without input tax credit

From 22 September 2025, beauty and physical well-being services, including salons, are taxed at 5% without input tax credit. Before that date the rate was 18%. CBIC has said the 5% rate is mandatory, so a salon cannot choose the old 18% option. In practice you cannot claim back the GST you pay on products, rent or equipment, so price your services with that cost in mind.

A service provider must register for GST once yearly turnover goes above ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special category states). Check any price with the free GST calculator, and if you make GST tax invoices for other businesses, use the GST invoice generator to get a PDF.

Mistakes to avoid on a parlour bill

  • Charging the old 18% rate. Check that your bill uses 5% now.
  • Not saying whether prices include GST. Put it on your price list and on the bill.
  • Skipping bill numbers. Keep one running series so no bill goes missing.
  • Not recording the stylist. You need it to work out commission and see which services earn the most.
  • Mixing GST and non-GST bills. If you are registered, every bill carries your GSTIN and the tax split.

Frequently asked questions

What should a salon bill include?

The salon name, address and phone, a running bill number and the date, the customer name, each service with its quantity and price, any discount, the GST amount if you are registered, the total, and the payment mode. If you are GST-registered, add your GSTIN.

What is the GST rate on salon and beauty parlour services?

Beauty and physical well-being services, including salons, are taxed at 5% without input tax credit from 22 September 2025. Before that date the rate was 18%. For a bill in the same state, 5% is split into 2.5% CGST and 2.5% SGST.

Do I need a GSTIN on my salon bill?

Only if you are registered for GST. Service providers must register once yearly turnover crosses ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special category states). If you are not registered, issue a plain bill without GST and leave the GSTIN out.

Can I use this template if I am not GST-registered?

Yes. Set the GST rate cell to 0 and leave the GSTIN blank. The template then shows a plain bill with no tax.

How do I work out the price before GST if my menu price includes GST?

Divide the price by 1.05. For example, a menu price of ₹1,890 including 5% GST is ₹1,800 before GST. The free GST calculator can do this for any rate.
