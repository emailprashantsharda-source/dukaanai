How to calculate a discount

Discount amount = Original price × Discount % ÷ 100, and Price after discount = Original price − Discount amount.

Example: a ₹1,500 item at 20% off has a discount of ₹1,500 × 20 ÷ 100 = ₹300, so the price after discount is ₹1,200.

How to find the discount percentage

Discount % = (Original price − Sale price) ÷ Original price × 100.

Example: an item marked ₹1,299 that sells for ₹999 has a discount of ₹300, which is ₹300 ÷ ₹1,299 × 100 = about 23.1%.

How to find the original price before a discount

Original price = Sale price ÷ (1 − Discount % ÷ 100).

Example: a shirt costs ₹800 after a 20% discount, so the original price was ₹800 ÷ 0.80 = ₹1,000.

Two discounts in a row are not added together

The second discount applies to the price that is left after the first. On ₹1,000, 20% off leaves ₹800, and a further 10% off leaves ₹720. The total discount is 28%, not 30%. Use the second discount box in the calculator to check offers like "20% off plus 10% extra".

Discount and GST

When you give a discount at the time of sale and show it on the invoice, it is deducted from the value of the supply, so GST is charged on the price after the discount. Take the ₹1,500 item at 20% off: the price after discount is ₹1,200, 18% GST on that is ₹216 (₹108 CGST and ₹108 SGST), and the customer pays ₹1,416. The GST rate depends on the item, so pick the right one in the calculator. For the full tax split, use the GST calculator. Discounts given after the sale have extra conditions, so ask your CA.

What a discount costs a shop

A discount comes straight out of your profit, not out of your cost. Say an item costs you ₹800 and sells for ₹1,000: your profit is ₹200 and your margin is 20%. A 10% discount drops the price to ₹900. Your cost is still ₹800, so profit falls to ₹100 and margin to 11.1%. A 10% discount has taken away half of your profit on that item. Before you run a sale, check the margin on the sale price with the profit margin calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount?

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage and divide by 100 to get the discount amount, then subtract it from the original price. A ₹1,500 item at 20% off has a discount of ₹300, so the price after discount is ₹1,200.

How do I find the discount percentage from two prices?

Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide by the original price and multiply by 100. For an original price of ₹1,299 and a sale price of ₹999, the discount is ₹300, which is about 23.1%.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the sale price by (1 minus the discount as a fraction). If a shirt costs ₹800 after a 20% discount, the original price is ₹800 ÷ 0.80 = ₹1,000.

Is a 20% and then a 10% discount the same as 30% off?

No. The second discount applies to the already reduced price. On ₹1,000, 20% off gives ₹800, and 10% off that gives ₹720, a total discount of 28%, not 30%.

Is GST charged on the price before or after the discount?

When the discount is given at the time of sale and shown on the invoice, it is deducted from the value of the supply, so GST is charged on the price after the discount. In the example, 18% GST on ₹1,200 is ₹216, so the customer pays ₹1,416. Confirm the rules for your case with your CA.
