How to Do Menu Costing: A Comprehensive Guide

By HisaabNow Team · 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

Discover effective strategies for menu costing to maximize your restaurant's profitability, including practical examples and tips.

Understanding Menu Costing

Menu costing is the process of calculating the total cost of each dish on your restaurant's menu. It is essential for effective restaurant management. Proper menu costing helps you understand how much each item costs to prepare, which directly influences your pricing strategy.

Menu costing involves several key components:

  • Labor Costs: This refers to the wages of the staff involved in preparing and serving the food. If the preparation of a dish takes a certain amount of time and the average wage is a specific amount per hour, the labor cost for that dish would be calculated based on these figures.
  • Overhead Costs: These are the indirect costs of running your restaurant, such as rent, utilities, and equipment maintenance. If your monthly overhead is a certain amount and you serve a specific number of meals, each meal contributes to overhead costs based on this calculation.

By understanding these components, you can create a well-structured pricing strategy that aligns with your business goals.

Calculating Food Costs

Calculating food costs is essential for setting profitable menu prices. Start by determining the cost of each ingredient in a dish. For this, you need to know the price per unit of each ingredient and the quantity used in the recipe.

Here’s a simple formula to calculate the total food cost per dish:

  • Total Food Cost = Sum of (Cost of each ingredient × Quantity used)

Once you have the total food cost, divide this by the number of servings the dish provides to find the cost per serving:

  • Cost per Serving = Total Food Cost ÷ Number of Servings

This means each serving costs you a certain amount in ingredients. Knowing this helps you set a price that covers costs and generates profit.

Including Overhead and Labor Costs

When setting menu prices, it’s essential to include overhead and labor costs to ensure your dishes are profitable.

Factoring in Overhead Costs

Overhead costs are expenses that keep your restaurant running but aren’t directly tied to any specific dish. This includes rent, utilities, and equipment maintenance. To factor these into your menu costing, start by calculating your total monthly overhead. Then, divide this amount by the number of dishes you serve in a month. This gives you an overhead cost per dish.

Add these costs together to find the total overhead cost per dish.

Calculating Labor Costs

Next, calculate labor costs based on preparation time and staff wages. For example, if a dish takes 30 minutes to prepare and your labor cost is ₹200 per hour, the labor cost per dish is ₹100. This is calculated as follows:

  • 30 minutes is half an hour.

By adding the overhead cost and labor cost, the total cost to prepare this dish would be calculated. Use this total to help set your menu prices effectively.

Setting Menu Prices

Setting menu prices is crucial for your restaurant's success. You need to ensure that your prices cover total costs and provide a profit margin. Start by calculating the total cost of each dish, including food costs, overhead, and labor. Once you have this figure, decide on your desired profit margin.

  • Menu price = Total cost + Desired profit. You may choose to round up the price for easier customer perception.

Next, consider market rates and competitor pricing. Research similar dishes in your area. If competitors charge for a comparable dish, you might want to adjust your price accordingly. This helps you stay competitive while still achieving your desired margin.

It's important to strike a balance. If your prices are too high, you risk losing customers. If they're too low, you may not cover your costs. Regularly review your pricing strategy to ensure it aligns with market trends and your business goals.

Monitoring and Adjusting Costs

Monitoring and adjusting costs is crucial for keeping your restaurant profitable. Regularly reviewing your menu costs and prices helps you stay in tune with your expenses and market conditions. If the price of ingredients goes up, it might be time to adjust your menu prices accordingly.

Seasonal changes can also affect your menu costs. This is a great time to promote dishes featuring that fruit at a lower price, attracting customers while keeping costs down.

Keep an eye on your inventory and ingredient usage. If certain items are consistently costing more, consider substituting them with alternatives that provide similar quality at a lower price. Always communicate with your suppliers to negotiate better rates or find more cost-effective options.

Practical Checklist for Menu Costing

Here's a practical checklist to help you with menu costing:

  • Calculate Food Costs: Start by determining the cost of each ingredient in your dishes. This includes everything from spices to main components.
  • Include Overhead: Don’t forget to factor in overhead costs like rent, utilities, and labor. These are essential for understanding your total expenses.
  • Set Prices: Based on your calculated costs, set menu prices that ensure a reasonable profit margin. Remember to consider your target market.
  • Monitor Regularly: Keep an eye on ingredient prices and market trends. Adjust your menu and prices as needed to stay profitable.
