Beauty Parlour Business Plan: Costs, Break-Even and Licences

By HisaabNow Team · 12 November 2026 · 8 min read

A beauty parlour business plan is a short document that tells you how much money you need, what you must earn each month to cover costs, and which licences you need before you open. This guide shows each part with worked figures in rupees.

Quick answer: a salon business plan has six parts. 1) Your services, customers and location. 2) Licences and GST. 3) Start-up costs. 4) Monthly costs and a break-even figure. 5) Pricing for each service. 6) A daily routine to track sales and profit. In our example, a small parlour needs about ₹3,75,000 to start and about ₹54,100 of service revenue a month to break even. These are example figures, so replace them with your own quotes. Updated 12 November 2026.

What a beauty parlour business plan includes

A parlour business plan answers five questions before you spend money: who will visit you, which services you will offer, how much you need to start, how much you must earn each month just to cover your costs, and how you will keep track of it all after you open. It usually fits on two or three pages, and the numbers matter more than the wording.

The sections below follow that order. If you also plan to sell products such as hair or skin care items, keep their costs in a separate line so you can see which part of the business earns the profit.

Step 1: Decide your services, customers and location

Start with the services you can deliver well on day one, for example haircuts, threading, facials, waxing and bridal makeup. A short list you do well is better than a long list you do badly, and you can add services once the first customers return.

Then look at who lives and works near your shop. A parlour near a market serves homemaker and festival customers, while one near offices serves quick services such as threading and hair styling. Visit two or three parlours nearby, note their service list and prices, and decide what you will do differently: a cleaner shop, a female-only team, evening hours or home visits.

Step 2: Licences, registrations and GST

The licences you need depend on your state and municipality, so confirm the list with your local municipal office before you sign a lease. A typical list for a salon includes:

  • Trade licence from your local municipality.
  • Shops and Establishments registration, which comes under your state's law.
  • GST registration, but only once your yearly turnover crosses the limit below.
  • Fire safety clearance for larger premises, with the size limit set by your state.

For GST, a service provider must register when yearly turnover goes above ₹20 lakh in most states, or ₹10 lakh in special category states. Salon and other beauty and physical well-being services are taxed at 5% without input tax credit from 22 September 2025, and CBIC has said this rate is mandatory. In practice, you cannot claim back the GST you pay on products and equipment, so include that cost in your budget and prices. You can check any amount with the free GST calculator.

Step 3: Start-up costs for a small parlour

Here is an example for a small parlour with two chairs and two staff members. Costs vary a lot by city, shop size and brands, so treat every number as a placeholder and get real quotes.

ItemExample cost
Chairs, mirrors, trolleys and basic furniture₹1,20,000
Tools and equipment (dryers, steamer, tools, sterilising kit)₹60,000
Opening stock of products and consumables₹40,000
Rent deposit (3 months of rent)₹45,000
Licences, registrations and signage₹10,000
Working-capital cushion for the first months₹1,00,000
Total start-up money₹3,75,000

Do not skip the working-capital cushion. In the first months you will pay rent and salaries before regular customers build up, and that cash has to come from somewhere.

Step 4: Monthly costs and your break-even figure

Break-even is the amount of service revenue you must earn each month to cover all your costs. Start with the costs that stay the same whether you have one customer or fifty.

Fixed cost (example)Per month
Shop rent₹15,000
Salaries (2 staff at ₹12,000)₹24,000
Electricity and water₹4,000
Phone, cleaning and local promotion₹3,000
Fixed costs a month₹46,000

Some costs rise with every service you sell: creams, colours, wax, disposable items. Suppose these use up 15% of your service revenue. That leaves 85% of each rupee to pay your fixed costs.

Break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ (1 − product cost share)

With the example figures: ₹46,000 ÷ 0.85 = about ₹54,100 a month. Over 26 working days that is about ₹2,081 a day. If your average bill is ₹600, you need about 3.5 paying customers a day, so plan for 4, just to cover costs. These figures exclude GST and your own pay.

Now see what happens in a good month. At ₹90,000 of revenue (about 150 bills of ₹600, or 5.8 a day), product costs are ₹13,500, fixed costs are ₹46,000, and what is left is about ₹30,500. At that rate the ₹3,75,000 you put in comes back in roughly 12 months, before you pay yourself. If your plan shows revenue below break-even, change the plan, for example a cheaper location or fewer staff at first, before you spend money.

Step 5: Price each service so it earns a margin

Price from cost, not from what the parlour next door charges. For each service, add the products it uses, the staff time it takes and a share of rent and electricity, then add the margin you want. A facial that uses ₹120 of products and takes 45 minutes of a stylist's time costs you more than it looks like. Check each service with the free profit margin calculator, which shows margin and markup from cost and selling price.

Remember that salon services carry 5% GST. Decide whether your listed prices include it and say so clearly on the price list and on the bill.

Checklist for your beauty parlour business plan

  • Services and customers: a short list of services, and who near you will buy them.
  • Licences: trade licence and Shops and Establishments registration confirmed with your municipality and state.
  • GST: know the turnover limit, and the 5% rate without input tax credit.
  • Start-up budget: every item quoted, plus a cash cushion.
  • Break-even: fixed costs ÷ (1 − product cost share), turned into customers per day.
  • Prices: each service priced with a margin, and a clear GST note.
  • Tracking: a billing app set up before the first customer walks in.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start a beauty parlour?

It depends on your city, shop size and brands. In the example above, a small two-chair parlour needs about ₹3,75,000 including a cash cushion. Get quotes for your own items before you decide.

Which licences does a beauty parlour need?

Usually a trade licence from your municipality and a Shops and Establishments registration under your state's law. GST registration is needed once turnover crosses the limit. Rules differ by state, so confirm with your local municipal office.

What is the GST rate on salon services?

Beauty and physical well-being services, including salons, are taxed at 5% without input tax credit from 22 September 2025.

How do I calculate break-even for a salon?

Add all fixed monthly costs, then divide by the share of each rupee left after product costs. In our example, ₹46,000 ÷ 0.85 is about ₹54,100 a month.

Do I need billing software for a small parlour?

You can start with a notebook, but billing software shows profit per service and per stylist from the first day, which makes it much easier to check your plan against real numbers.
