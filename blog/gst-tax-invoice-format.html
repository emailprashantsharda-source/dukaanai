Understanding GST Tax Invoice Format

By HisaabNow Team · 21 November 2026 · 6 min read

This article explains the GST tax invoice format, its essential fields, and practical examples to help Indian shop owners comply with GST regulations.

What is a GST Tax Invoice?

A GST tax invoice is a document that businesses issue when they sell goods or services. It serves as proof of the transaction and includes details about the sale, such as the items sold, their prices, and the applicable GST rates. This invoice is crucial for businesses because it helps them comply with tax regulations and maintain accurate records.

There are two main types of invoices in the GST system: a tax invoice and a bill of supply. A tax invoice is issued for taxable supplies, where GST is charged. On the other hand, a bill of supply is used for exempt supplies, meaning no GST is charged.

Businesses need to issue a GST tax invoice under specific circumstances. If the sale value is ₹50,000 or more and the buyer is unregistered, the invoice must also include the buyer's name and address. Additionally, the tax invoice must be issued at the time of delivery or service. This ensures that both the seller and buyer have a clear record of the transaction, which is essential for filing taxes and claiming input tax credits.

Essential Fields in a GST Tax Invoice

When issuing a GST tax invoice, including the essential fields is crucial for compliance and clarity. Here are the key elements you need to ensure are present:

  • Supplier's Information: Clearly state your name, address, and GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number). This helps identify your business and ensures proper tax reporting.
  • Invoice Details: Each invoice must have a unique consecutive serial number for that financial year. This means if your first invoice is numbered 001, the next should be 002, and so on. Also, include the date of the invoice to indicate when the sale occurred.
  • HSN/SAC Code: Include the HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) or SAC (Service Accounting Code) for the goods or services sold. This helps in correctly categorizing the items for GST purposes.

Understanding GST Rates and Splits

Understanding GST rates is crucial for shop owners. Since 22 September 2025, the main GST slabs are 0%, 5%, 18%, and 40%. These rates apply to various goods and services. For example, essential items like certain food products may have a 0% GST rate, while luxury goods could be taxed at 40%.

GST is split into three components: CGST, SGST, and IGST. For sales within the same state (intra-state), GST is divided equally between CGST and SGST.

In contrast, for inter-state sales, you charge IGST at the full rate. If you sell the same product to a customer in another state, you would charge ₹100 as IGST (5%).

Understanding these splits helps ensure compliance and accurate billing, making it easier to manage your tax obligations.

Handling Exempt Supplies

If you sell goods that are exempt from GST, you will issue a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice. For example, if you sell a book for ₹500, you would not charge GST. If you have both taxable and exempt items in one sale, you can issue an invoice-cum-bill of supply.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in GST Invoices

  • Missing Mandatory Fields: Every GST invoice must include specific details. These include the supplier's name, address, and GSTIN, as well as a unique invoice number and date. If any of these fields are missing, the invoice may not be valid.
  • Incorrect GST Rates: Applying the wrong GST rate to items is a frequent error. For example, if you sell a product that is taxable at 5% but mistakenly apply 18%, it can create discrepancies in your tax filings. Always verify the GST rate based on the HSN code of the item.
  • Wrong Invoice Type for Exempt Goods/Services: If you sell exempt goods or services, you must issue a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice. Failing to do this can lead to penalties.

By paying attention to these common mistakes, you can ensure that your GST invoicing is accurate and compliant with regulations. This will save you time and potential fines in the long run.

Checklist for Issuing GST Tax Invoices

Checklist for Issuing GST Tax Invoices

Issuing GST tax invoices can be straightforward if you follow a checklist. Here are the key points to remember:

  • Required Fields: Make sure your invoice includes all necessary fields. This includes your name, address, GSTIN, invoice number, date, recipient details, HSN code, item description, quantity, total value, and applicable GST rates.
  • GST Rates and HSN Codes: Double-check the GST rate and HSN codes for each item. Incorrect rates can lead to compliance issues. You can verify these rates on the CBIC rate finder or consult your CA.
  • Record Keeping: Keep a record of all issued invoices. This will help you during GST filing and ensure compliance with tax regulations.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a tax invoice and a bill of supply?

A tax invoice is issued for taxable goods and services, while a bill of supply is issued for exempt goods or services.

Do unregistered businesses need to issue GST invoices?

Unregistered businesses cannot charge GST or issue tax invoices. They can issue a simple bill instead.

What happens if I make a mistake on a GST invoice?

If there's a mistake, you can issue a credit note to correct it and inform the buyer.

How can I ensure my invoices are GST compliant?

Make sure to include all required fields and apply the correct GST rates as per the latest regulations.
