How to Create a GST Invoice Format for Your Shop

By HisaabNow Team · 26 September 2026 · 8 min read

Learn to create GST-compliant invoices, essential for Indian shop owners. Ensure accuracy and compliance with these tips.

Understanding GST Invoice Requirements

A GST invoice is a crucial document for any Indian shop owner. It serves as proof of transaction and is essential for tax purposes. A well-prepared GST invoice not only ensures compliance with tax regulations but also helps in maintaining clear records for both the seller and the buyer.

To create a compliant GST invoice, certain mandatory fields must be included. Firstly, the GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) of the supplier is essential. This unique number is necessary for identifying the registered taxpayer. Additionally, the HSN code (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) is required to classify the goods being sold. For example, if you are selling sweets, you will need to enter the correct HSN code for those items.

Another key component is the taxable value of the goods or services provided. This is the amount on which GST is calculated. It's important to break down the GST into its components: CGST (Central Goods and Services Tax), SGST (State Goods and Services Tax), and IGST (Integrated Goods and Services Tax), depending on whether the sale is intra-state or inter-state. For instance, if you are selling within Maharashtra, you will need to include both CGST and SGST details.

Ensuring these elements are present and accurate on your GST invoice helps in avoiding penalties and facilitates smooth business operations. Always check the current rules on the GST portal to stay updated with any changes in requirements.

Essential Elements of a GST Invoice

Creating a GST invoice that complies with Indian tax regulations is crucial for your shop. Let's break down the essential elements you need to include.

Invoice Number and Date: Every GST invoice must have a unique invoice number. This helps in tracking and referencing the invoice easily. The date of issue should also be clearly mentioned for record-keeping purposes.

Supplier Details: Your shop's name, address, and GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) must be included on the invoice. This ensures transparency and accountability in your transactions.

Buyer's GSTIN and Address: If your buyer is registered under GST, include their GSTIN and address. This is important for their input tax credit claims. It also helps in maintaining accurate transaction records.

Item Description and Quantity: Clearly describe each item sold, including the quantity. For instance, if you sell 10 packets of biscuits at ₹50 each, mention this detail. This transparency helps both you and your buyer understand the transaction better and ensures accurate tax calculation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Creating a GST invoice might seem straightforward, but common mistakes can lead to compliance issues and financial discrepancies. One frequent error is entering an incorrect GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number). A wrong GSTIN can cause the invoice to be invalid, affecting your ability to claim input tax credit. Always double-check the GSTIN before finalizing the invoice.

Another common mistake is omitting HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) codes. These codes classify goods and services for GST purposes. Missing or incorrect HSN codes can lead to penalties or rejections during GST filing. Ensure that each product or service on your invoice has the correct HSN code.

Errors in tax calculations are also problematic. Miscalculating GST can result in either undercharging or overcharging your customers. For example, if the GST rate is 18% (check the current rule on the GST portal) and you mistakenly apply a different rate, it could lead to discrepancies in your tax liabilities. Use reliable software or a calculator to ensure accuracy.

Examples of GST Invoice Formats

Creating a GST invoice can seem daunting, but with the right format, it's straightforward. Let's explore some examples tailored to different shop types.

Simple GST Invoice

A simple GST invoice is ideal for small transactions. For instance, a grocery shop might list items like rice, dal, and sugar with their quantities and prices. Each entry should include the HSN code and applicable GST rate. For example, a packaged bag of rice at ₹500 with a 5% GST rate would add ₹25 as tax, making the total ₹525.

Detailed GST Invoice

For larger transactions, a detailed invoice is necessary. A restaurant might need to include each dish, its price, applicable CGST and SGST, and a total. For example, a ₹1,000 meal at 5% GST is split into 2.5% CGST (₹25) and 2.5% SGST (₹25), making the total ₹1,050 (check the current rule on the GST portal).

Shop-Specific Formats

  • Restaurants: Include table numbers, waiter names, and detailed item descriptions.
  • Grocery Stores: Use a format that allows quick entry of multiple items with varying GST rates.
  • Salons: List services provided, stylist names, and any applicable discounts or packages.

Using templates ensures consistency and saves time. Templates can be customized to include your shop's logo, address, and contact details, maintaining a professional look across all invoices. This consistency helps in maintaining clear records and simplifies accounting.

Checklist for Creating a GST Invoice

Creating a GST invoice can seem daunting, but a systematic approach ensures accuracy and compliance. Here's a checklist to guide you:

  1. Include Mandatory Fields: Ensure the invoice has your shop's GSTIN, the buyer's GSTIN (if applicable), invoice number, date, and item details with HSN codes.
  2. Check Tax Calculations: Verify that CGST, SGST, or IGST amounts are correctly calculated and displayed. For example, if an item costs ₹1,000 with 18% GST, ensure ₹90 each for CGST and SGST are added.
  3. Ensure Clarity: Clearly mention the total amount, including taxes. Avoid rounding errors by using a reliable billing system.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CGST, SGST, and IGST?

CGST and SGST are levied on intra-state sales, while IGST is for inter-state sales. Each has specific rates (check the current rule on the GST portal).

How often should I update my invoice format?

Update your invoice format whenever there are changes in GST rules or your business details to ensure compliance.

Can I issue a GST invoice without a GSTIN?

No, a GSTIN is mandatory for issuing GST invoices. Register for GST to obtain one.

Do I issue a GST invoice for exempt items?

No. For exempt items you issue a bill of supply, not a tax invoice. If one bill has both taxable and exempt items, a combined invoice-cum-bill of supply can be used (check the current rule on the GST portal).
