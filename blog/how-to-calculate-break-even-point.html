How to Calculate Break Even Point

By HisaabNow Team · 28 November 2026 · 8 min read

Understanding how to calculate the break even point is crucial for your shop's profitability. This guide offers clear steps and practical examples.

Understanding Break Even Point

The break even point is the point at which your total revenue equals your total costs. This means you are not making a profit, but you are also not losing money. Understanding your break even point is crucial for small businesses. It helps you know how much you need to sell to cover your costs and start making a profit.

Costs in a business can be divided into two main categories: fixed costs and variable costs. Fixed costs are expenses that remain constant, regardless of how much you sell. Even if you sell nothing, you still have to pay these bills.

On the other hand, variable costs change with the level of sales. These include costs like materials, packaging, or direct labor. The more sweets you sell, the more sugar and flour you need to purchase.

Calculating your break even point helps you make informed decisions. It allows you to set sales targets and understand the impact of cost changes on your profitability. For example, if you find out your break even point is ₹50,000 in sales, you can work towards that target to ensure your business stays afloat. Knowing this number can help you adjust your pricing or reduce costs to improve your margins.

Worked example: Break Even Calculation

Let’s look at a worked example to understand how to calculate the break-even point. Suppose a kirana store has fixed costs every month totaling ₹32,000. This includes rent, staff salaries, electricity, and other running costs.

Each average bill in the store sells for ₹400, and the cost of the goods sold in that bill is ₹320. This means that for each bill, the store contributes ₹80 towards covering the fixed costs. Here’s how we calculate it step by step:

  1. Identify fixed costs: The total fixed costs are ₹32,000.
  2. Calculate contribution margin: Subtract the cost of goods sold from the sales price: ₹400 (sales price) - ₹320 (cost) = ₹80.
  3. Calculate break-even point: Divide the total fixed costs by the contribution margin: ₹32,000 ÷ ₹80 = 400 average bills.

This means the store needs to sell 400 average bills each month to break even. If it sells less than this, it incurs a loss. Selling more than 400 bills means the store starts making a profit, adding ₹80 for each additional bill sold. This analysis helps shop owners make informed decisions about pricing and sales targets. For further calculations, you can use the profit margin calculator.

Break-even point = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost) = ₹32,000 ÷ ₹80 = 400 average bills a month.

That is 400 × ₹400 = ₹1,60,000 of sales a month. Below that the kirana store makes a loss; every average bill above it adds ₹80 of profit.

Identifying Your Costs

Identifying your costs is crucial for calculating your break even point. Costs can be divided into two main categories: fixed costs and variable costs.

Fixed Costs

Fixed costs remain constant regardless of your sales volume. These are expenses you must pay even if you sell nothing. Examples include:

  • Rent: Suppose your shop pays for rent.
  • Salaries: If you have a staff of three and pay them, that totals monthly salaries.
  • Insurance: This could be around monthly insurance costs.

In this case, your total fixed costs would be calculated based on the identified fixed costs.

Variable Costs

Variable costs fluctuate based on your sales. These are directly tied to the products or services you provide. Examples include:

  • Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): If you sell sweets, the ingredients might cost you ₹20,000 a month.
  • Packaging: If you spend on boxes and bags for your products.

Here, your total variable costs could be calculated based on the identified variable costs.

To track these costs effectively, maintain a detailed record of all expenses. Use accounting software or a simple spreadsheet. Update it regularly to reflect any changes.

Accurate cost assessment is vital. This can lead to financial trouble. Knowing your true costs helps you set the right sales price and plan for profitability.

Sales Price and Cost of Goods Sold

Determining your sales price and the cost of goods sold (COGS) is crucial for calculating your break even point. The sales price is the amount you charge customers for your product or service. COGS includes all direct costs associated with producing or purchasing your goods, such as raw materials and labor.

To find your sales price, consider what similar products in your market are selling for. You may also factor in your target profit margin.

Pricing strategies can significantly impact your break even point. A higher sales price can lower the number of units you need to sell to break even. Conversely, a lower sales price might attract more customers but could require selling more units to cover costs.

For example, suppose your COGS is ₹300, and you sell your product for ₹500. Your profit per unit is ₹200. To break even on your fixed costs of ₹10,000, you would need to sell 50 units (₹10,000 ÷ ₹200). If you decide to lower your price to ₹450, your profit per unit drops to ₹150. In this case, you would need to sell 67 units to break even (₹10,000 ÷ ₹150). This shows how small adjustments in pricing can affect your profitability and the number of sales needed to reach break even.

Using Break Even Analysis for Business Decisions

Break even analysis is a powerful tool for making informed business decisions. One of its key uses is in setting prices. By knowing your break even point, you can determine the minimum price you need to charge to cover costs.

Additionally, break even analysis can help with inventory management. For instance, if your analysis shows you need to sell 300 units to break even, you can avoid overstocking items that are not selling well. This prevents unnecessary costs and waste.

Break even analysis is also critical when considering new products or services. Suppose you want to launch a new dessert in your café. Before investing in ingredients and marketing, calculate the break even point. If it’s too high compared to expected sales, you might reconsider the launch or adjust your pricing strategy.

In summary, use break even analysis for pricing decisions, inventory management, and evaluating new offerings. It provides clarity and helps you make data-driven decisions to enhance your business's profitability.

Checklist for Calculating Break Even Point

Calculating your break even point is essential for understanding your business's financial health. Here’s a checklist to help you through the process:

  • Identify Fixed Costs: List all your fixed costs, such as rent, salaries, and utilities. These costs do not change regardless of your sales volume.
  • Determine Variable Costs: Calculate the variable costs associated with each product or service you offer. This includes materials and direct labor.
  • Set Sales Price: Decide on the selling price for each item or service you provide.
  • Calculate Contribution Margin: Subtract variable costs from the sales price to find the contribution margin per unit.
  • Perform Break Even Calculation: Divide your total fixed costs by the contribution margin to find your break even point.

Maintain accurate records of your costs and sales to ensure your calculations are correct. Using tools like the profit margin calculator can simplify this process. Regularly review your break even analysis, especially as market conditions and costs change. This will help you make informed decisions for your business.
