Salon Price List: Sample Menu and How to Price Each Service

By HisaabNow Team · 12 November 2026 · 7 min read

A salon price list tells customers what each service costs, but the prices on it have to cover your products, your staff time and your rent. This guide shows what to put on the list, and how to work out each price from cost.

Quick answer: price each salon service from cost, not from the parlour next door. Cost = products used + staff time + a share of rent and electricity. Then price = cost ÷ (1 − the margin you want). In our example parlour, a basic facial costs about ₹519 to deliver, so at a 40% margin it must be priced near ₹865 before GST. These are example figures, so replace them with your own. Updated 12 November 2026.

What a salon price list should include

Whether you call it a salon price list, a beauty parlour price list or a parlour rate list, it is the same one-page menu of services and prices that customers see at the counter.

  • Service name, in words customers use (haircut, threading, facial) and not only your internal names.
  • Time each service takes, so customers can plan and you can check your costs.
  • Price, with a clear note on whether it includes GST.
  • Packages, such as bridal or monthly care, priced as a whole.
  • The date the list was updated, so an old list never causes a dispute.

Keep it to one page. A long list is hard to read and hard to keep up to date.

How to price a salon service

The method has three steps. It follows the cost-first approach that other salon guides recommend too, such as Dingg's pricing guide, which suggests aiming for a margin of roughly 25% to 40% on each service.

  1. Cost the service. Add the products it uses, the staff time it takes, and a share of your rent, electricity and other fixed costs.
  2. Add your margin. Price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). For a 40% margin, divide the cost by 0.60.
  3. Check the local market. Compare with three or four parlours near you. Your cost-based price is the floor: if the market pays less, fix the cost or the service rather than selling below cost.

Worked example: three services in one parlour

We use the same example parlour as in our beauty parlour business plan: a stylist paid ₹12,000 a month for 208 hours of work, and ₹22,000 a month of rent, electricity and other fixed costs spread over about 156 services of 40 minutes. That makes one minute of service time cost about ₹4.49 (₹0.96 of staff time plus ₹3.53 of fixed costs).

Show GST clearly

Salon and other beauty and physical well-being services are taxed at 5% without input tax credit from 22 September 2025. That means the GST you pay on products and equipment is a cost you cannot claim back, so build it into your prices. Decide whether your list shows prices with or without GST, and say so on the list and on every bill.

If your menu price includes GST, divide by 1.05 to find the price before GST. A facial listed at ₹910 is ₹867 before GST plus ₹43 of GST. The free GST calculator does this for any rate, and our salon bill format shows how the GST appears on the bill.

Packages and discounts

A package must still earn a margin. Take the three services above together: they cost about ₹853 to deliver and sell for ₹1,422 before GST. A package at 10% off is ₹1,280, which still leaves a margin of about 33%. A package at 30% off would leave far less, so check the numbers before you advertise a discount.

Keep your prices up to date

Review your list whenever your costs change: a rent increase, a salary change, a new product supplier or a tax change. Put the update date on the list. A profit report by service shows which prices are too low: if a service sells well but earns little, raise the price or lower its product cost.

Checklist for your salon price list

  • Costs: product, time and fixed cost worked out for each service.
  • Margin: a target margin chosen, and each price calculated as cost ÷ (1 − margin).
  • Market: prices compared with three or four nearby parlours.
  • GST: 5% shown clearly, with a note on whether prices include it.
  • Packages: each checked so it still earns a margin.
  • Date: the update date printed on the list.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a salon price list?

List each service with its time and price, add a note on GST, group packages separately and print the update date. Work out each price from cost, then compare it with nearby parlours.

How do I price a salon service?

Add the cost of products, staff time and a share of fixed costs, then divide by (1 − your target margin). For a 40% margin, divide the cost by 0.60.

What margin should a salon service have?

One salon pricing guide suggests about 25% to 40% per service. The right figure for you depends on your costs and your market, so check each service against your own numbers.

Should salon prices include GST?

Either way works, as long as the list and the bill say which one you use. Salon services carry 5% GST without input tax credit from 22 September 2025, so make sure that cost is inside your prices.

What is a beauty parlour price list or parlour rate list?

It is the menu of services, times and prices shown to customers. The two names mean the same thing: use whichever your customers use, and keep it to one page with the update date printed on it.

How often should I update my salon price list?

Whenever your costs or tax rules change, and at least once a year. Print the date on the list.
