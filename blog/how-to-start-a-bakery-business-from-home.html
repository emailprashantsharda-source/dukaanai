How to Start a Bakery Business from Home in India

By HisaabNow Team · 1 November 2026 · 9 min read

A home bakery needs a small menu, an FSSAI registration, a price that covers your time, and a simple way to take orders. This guide walks through each step for India, with a worked pricing example.

Quick answer: to start a bakery business from home in India: 1) pick three to five products and test them with real customers. 2) Register with FSSAI before you sell (basic registration if your yearly turnover is under ₹12 lakh). 3) Cost every item including your time, then price it for the margin you want. 4) Take orders on WhatsApp with an advance for custom cakes. 5) Check whether GST applies to you. Updated 1 November 2026.

1. Start with a small menu

Pick three to five products you can make well every time, such as a chocolate cake, cupcakes, cookies and brownies. A small menu means fewer ingredients to stock, less waste and faster work. Bake each item for friends and neighbours first and ask what they would pay. Keep a note of what sells.

Custom birthday cakes bring higher prices but need more time and advance planning. Cookies and brownies are easier to repeat. Many home bakers begin with both.

2. Register with FSSAI before you sell

Selling food in India needs an FSSAI registration or licence, and this applies to home bakers too. The type depends on your yearly turnover:

  • Below ₹12 lakh a year: basic registration (Form A).
  • Above ₹12 lakh, up to ₹20 crore a year: a state licence (Form B).

Apply on the FSSAI Food Licensing and Registration portal. Fees and documents can change, so read the current list on the portal. Food delivery apps ask for your FSSAI number before they list you, so register before you plan to sell online. Selling without it can bring a fine or an order to stop.

3. Set up your kitchen and equipment

Keep baking separate from daily cooking where you can: a clean counter, covered storage for flour and sugar, and a fridge shelf for butter, cream and eggs. You need an oven you can rely on, an accurate weighing scale, mixing bowls, baking tins, a hand or stand mixer and food-safe packaging. Buy what your first menu needs and add equipment as orders grow. Wash hands, keep pets out of the kitchen and label every pack with the date it was made.

4. Cost every item and set your price

Most home bakers underprice because they leave their own time out. For each item, add up four things: ingredients, packaging, gas or electricity, and your time at an hourly rate you would accept. The total is your cost. Then pick the margin you want and divide:

Selling price = cost ÷ (1 − margin)

Example with illustrative numbers (replace them with your own) for a 1 kg chocolate cake:

ItemAmount
Ingredients₹380
Box and cake board₹40
Gas and electricity₹30
Your time: 2 hours at ₹100 an hour₹200
Total cost₹650
Price for a 30% margin: ₹650 ÷ 0.70₹928.57, round to ₹930
Profit at ₹930₹280, which is 30% of the price

Margin and markup are not the same. Adding 30% on top of the cost gives ₹845, and the profit of ₹195 is only 23% of that price. Use the profit margin calculator to check any price, and read how food costing works for the same idea applied to a restaurant menu. Re-check your costs every few months, because flour, butter and gas prices move.

5. Check whether GST applies to you

GST registration is required when your yearly turnover crosses the threshold. If you only supply goods, as a baker selling cakes and cookies does, the usual limit is ₹40 lakh, and ₹20 lakh in special category states. If you sell outside your state or through some online platforms, ask your CA, because other rules can apply.

On rates: since 22 September 2025, cakes and pastries are taxed at 5% (earlier 18%) and plain bread at 0%. Confirm the rate for your exact product before you invoice. If you are registered, your bill must show your GSTIN, the HSN code and the tax split; the GST invoice format guide lists every field, and the free GST invoice generator makes the bill for you.

6. Take orders and get paid

  • Orders: WhatsApp works well. Send a short menu with prices and the lead time for each item, for example 2 days for custom cakes.
  • Advance: ask for part payment on custom orders so a cancelled cake does not cost you the ingredients.
  • Payment: accept UPI and send a clear bill.
  • Order register: keep one line per order with the date, customer, item, advance paid and balance due.

When orders grow past what a notebook can hold, a billing app helps. HisaabNow for bakeries handles GST billing, stock and the profit on each item.

7. Grow without losing the margin

Ask every customer for a photo and a review, and offer a small discount on the next order for a referral. Add products only after your first menu sells steadily. Once a month, list your three best-selling items and check their real margin against the table above. Drop items that take many hours and earn little.

Launch checklist

  1. Choose three to five products and test them with ten customers.
  2. Apply for FSSAI basic registration (or a state licence if turnover will be higher).
  3. Set up a clean baking area and buy only the equipment your menu needs.
  4. Cost each item, including your time, and set prices for your target margin.
  5. Create a WhatsApp menu and an order register with advance and balance columns.
  6. Check the GST threshold and, if registered, issue proper GST bills.
  7. Review costs and best sellers every month.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an FSSAI licence to bake at home and sell?

Yes. Home bakers need an FSSAI registration. Basic registration (Form A) applies if your yearly turnover is under ₹12 lakh; above that you need a state licence (Form B). Check the current rules on the FSSAI portal.

Do I need GST registration for a home bakery?

Only if your yearly turnover from goods crosses ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh in special category states), or other rules apply to you, such as selling outside your state. Confirm with your CA.

How do I price cakes from home?

Add ingredients, packaging, gas or electricity and your own time to get the cost, then divide by (1 minus your target margin). A cost of ₹650 and a 30% margin gives a price of about ₹930.

How much money do I need to start?

It depends on your menu. Start with the oven and tools you already have, buy ingredients for your first few orders, and add equipment as sales come in.
